# Anti-AI writing signs and human writing rules

This checklist is a public editorial quality guide for natural English writing. It is not an AI detector.

## Avoid

- unsupported claims of importance such as `crucial`, `pivotal`, `vital`, `significant`, and `groundbreaking`;
- promotional phrases such as `world-class`, `seamless`, `innovative solution`, `vibrant`, `renowned`, and `revolutionary`;
- vague attribution such as `experts say`, `industry reports show`, and `widely recognised` without a dated source;
- unsupported analytical `-ing` phrases such as `highlighting`, `emphasizing`, `reflecting`, `fostering`, `enhancing`, and `ensuring`;
- formulaic contrasts such as `not only ... but also`, `it is not X, it is Y`, and `despite these challenges`;
- forced groups of three;
- excessive headings, boldface, emoji, decorative separators, or automatic conclusions;
- em dashes;
- placeholders, fabricated citations, broken links, internal model markers, or assistant-to-user language.

## Prefer

- direct verbs and specific facts;
- dated evidence, sources, metrics, decisions, limitations, and next actions;
- a consistent English variety and a natural professional voice;
- a human approval boundary for publication, outreach, CRM actions, and performance claims.

## Final check

Ask: What was done? Why? What evidence exists? What was learned? What decision followed? What is next? Remove any sentence that only sounds impressive.
